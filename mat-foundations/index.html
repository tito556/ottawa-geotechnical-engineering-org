Raft and Mat Foundation Design for Ottawa’s Sensitive Soils

Rigorous testing. Clear reporting.

LEARN MORE

Ottawa sits at about 70 metres above sea level on a complex bed of Champlain Sea sediments, and anyone who has excavated in the region knows what that means: Leda clay. With a population approaching 1.1 million in the metro area, the city keeps expanding into zones where this sensitive glaciomarine deposit governs foundation decisions. Raft or mat foundation design becomes a practical necessity when bearing capacity is marginal and differential settlement cannot be tolerated. In our experience, the conversation usually starts with a test pit investigation to map the weathered crust thickness, followed by targeted SPT drilling where the clay is deeper than expected. The raft slab bridges softer pockets while keeping contact pressures low, which is exactly what the soil conditions in Ottawa’s lowlands demand.

In Ottawa’s Leda clay, a well-designed raft slab doesn’t just support the building — it manages the settlement difference between the stiff crust and the soft layer beneath.

Methodology and scope

What we see repeatedly in Ottawa is that the stiffness contrast between the stiff upper crust and the underlying soft clay is sharper than the borehole logs suggest. A raft foundation distributes column loads across the entire footprint, reducing the risk of angular distortion in the superstructure. The design process here almost always includes a detailed assessment of frost heave — the National Building Code of Canada prescribes a minimum footing depth of 1.8 m in the region, but a structurally reinforced mat can be placed shallower when thermal insulation is incorporated. For projects near the Rideau River or in the Greenbelt, we often run a parallel liquefaction screening because loose alluvial lenses appear more frequently than the regional maps indicate. The mat thickness typically ranges from 400 mm to over 1 200 mm depending on column spacing and soil modulus, with reinforcement detailing governed by CSA A23.3-19. Where the clay is particularly compressible, we combine the mat with a vibrocompaction treatment to stiffen the upper few metres before formwork begins.

Local considerations

The most expensive error we see in Ottawa is treating a raft slab as a standard flat plate without accounting for the sensitivity of Leda clay. When the crust is broken through during excavation — something that happens easily with heavy equipment — the exposed soft clay remoulds almost instantly, losing up to 90 percent of its undisturbed strength. The result is not just additional settlement; it’s a sudden loss of subgrade support that a conventionally designed mat cannot bridge. A second common oversight is neglecting frost jacking at the slab perimeter, which can lift the edges unevenly and crack partition walls within the first two winters. Both scenarios are avoidable with a site-specific geotechnical model that includes the seasonal groundwater fluctuation measured in Ottawa’s monitoring wells and the real thickness of the stiff surficial layer confirmed by test pits every 15 to 20 metres across the footprint.

Frequently asked questions

At what depth do Ottawa soils require a raft foundation instead of isolated footings?

In Ottawa’s Leda clay belt, we generally start considering a raft when the stiff surface crust is thinner than about 2 metres and the underlying soft clay extends beyond 6 metres. Isolated footings in that profile tend to exceed the allowable bearing pressure or produce differential settlement above the 20 mm limit commonly adopted for masonry structures. The switch to a mat is a geotechnical decision, not a structural preference.

How do you handle the frost heave risk in a raft design under the National Building Code?

The NBCC 2020 requires foundation protection to at least 1.8 m depth in Ottawa, but a raft can be placed at a shallower elevation when rigid insulation is extended horizontally beyond the slab edge. We model the thermal gradient using local air-freezing index data and specify extruded polystyrene thicknesses that keep the subgrade above 0°C during a design winter. The frost skirt detail is reviewed against the Ontario Building Code supplement for unheated buildings if applicable.

What is the typical cost range for a raft foundation design in the Ottawa area?

A full raft foundation design package — covering geotechnical investigation coordination, soil-structure interaction analysis, and structural drawings — generally falls between CA$1,400 and CA$5,360, with the final figure depending on the building footprint, number of column loads, and whether supplementary ground improvement analysis is needed.

Can a raft foundation be combined with ground improvement in Ottawa’s sensitive clays?

Yes, and it’s a combination we specify regularly. When the crust is thin or disturbed, we often recommend vibrocompaction or rigid inclusions beneath the mat to create a stiffened transition layer. The raft then acts as a load-transfer platform, and the improved ground reduces both total and differential settlement. The key is performing pre- and post-treatment CPT testing to verify that the improvement has achieved the design modulus before the slab is cast.
