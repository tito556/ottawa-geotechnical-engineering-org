CPT (Cone Penetration Test) in Ottawa: Geotechnical Data for Sensitive Clays and Deep Foundations

Rigorous testing. Clear reporting.

LEARN MORE

The Leda clay that blankets much of the Ottawa Valley is among the most sensitive soils in Canada—lose its structure under load and shear strength drops by as much as 90%. Anyone who has cored through Champlain Sea deposits near the Rideau River knows the difference between a stiff dry crust and the liquefiable silts sitting right below the water table at 2.5 to 3.5 metres. That is precisely where a CPT (Cone Penetration Test) becomes indispensable. Instead of waiting for lab results from disturbed Shelby tube samples, the electric cone pushes through these layered profiles at a steady 20 mm/s, recording tip resistance, sleeve friction, and equilibrium pore pressure every 10 mm. For projects in liquefaction-prone zones east of downtown, we pair CPT soundings with pore dissipation tests to estimate in-situ permeability and drainage potential. The resulting log provides a nearly continuous profile that helps distinguish thin sand seams from the massive clays that define Ottawa's foundation risk.

A single CPT sounding near the Ottawa River can reveal three glacial cycles in 30 metres of log—no other field test captures that stratigraphic detail with such vertical resolution.

Scope of work

Ottawa's post-war expansion into neighborhoods like Alta Vista and Kanata spread infrastructure across terrain where the clay crust is only three to five metres thick, underlain by soft marine silts that extend 20 metres or more. Early developers often relied on standard penetration tests alone, but SPT blow counts in sensitive clay can be misleading—disturbance during driving masks the true undrained strength. Modern CPT (Cone Penetration Test) procedures have changed how we characterize these deposits. A typical Ottawa campaign starts with a 15 cm² electric cone pushed at the standard ASTM D5778 rate, with dissipation pauses at depths where pore pressure spikes suggest low-permeability lenses. The friction ratio profile separates organic silts from clean clay, and excess pore pressure ratio (Bq) flags zones where the soil is close to normally consolidated—meaning it never fully recovered from the last glacial retreat. For deep excavation design near the Confederation Line, we also integrate deep-excavations data with CPT-based soil behavior type charts to calibrate finite element models. The result is a ground model that reflects real stratigraphy, not just lab index tests on samples that may have swelled or cracked during recovery.

Area-specific notes

Contrast the sandy-floored bedrock valleys of Kanata with the deep clay troughs underlying Sandy Hill and Vanier, and the design implications of a CPT (Cone Penetration Test) become stark. In the west, refusal on limestone till may occur at 12 metres, while a borehole three kilometres east can still be advancing through soft Champlain clay at 30 metres without touching competent bearing strata. Misjudging that transition leads to differential settlement that no amount of structural reinforcement can fix after the fact. The bigger threat in Ottawa is post-peak strength loss in the sensitive clay matrix; if a slope cut or excavation unloads the formation too quickly, the undrained shear strength can collapse toward its fully remolded value, which is often less than 1 kPa. By running dissipation tests and mapping the preconsolidation pressure through net cone resistance, the CPT profile gives early warning of where this sensitivity is most acute, allowing foundation adjustments before construction mobilizes.

Common questions

What does a CPT (Cone Penetration Test) cost in the Ottawa region?

For a standard CPT sounding to 20-30 metres depth with pore pressure measurement and one or two dissipation tests, the typical range runs from CA$210 to CA$310 per metre mobilized. Mobilization, traffic control permits in downtown Ottawa, and specialized interpretation reports are priced separately. A full day of testing with three to five soundings is the most cost-efficient approach.

How does CPT compare to SPT for characterizing Ottawa's Leda clay?

SPT can overestimate undrained shear strength in sensitive clays because the driving disturbance remolds the soil around the sampler. The CPT measures in-situ response continuously, with no disturbance ahead of the cone. The friction ratio and pore pressure response also give direct indicators of sensitivity and overconsolidation ratio that SPT blow counts simply cannot provide.

Can CPT be used for seismic site classification in Ottawa?

Yes. When we run seismic cone (SCPTU) soundings, shear wave velocity is measured directly. Even without the seismic module, standard CPT data can estimate Vs through correlations calibrated to Champlain Sea deposits, allowing classification per NBCC 2020 site categories without a separate geophysical survey in many cases.
